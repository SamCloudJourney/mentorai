## 🔝 Top-6 — the concrete path & the single biggest risk

**1. SLI (Standard Lithium) — cleanest risk/reward.** A leveraged bet on lithium's cycle turn (carbonate ~$23k/t vs a ~$13k Dec-2025 trough) *plus* a Smackover DLE Final Investment Decision — but with the thing most lithium juniors lack: a **fixed, survivable balance sheet** ($141M cash, zero debt, Equinor co-funding, a $225M DOE grant with NEPA cleared, a binding Trafigura offtake). Base case it re-rates ~50% on FID; the 4–5x+ needs lithium running into the 2027–28 deficit while DLE de-risks. **Biggest risk:** FID almost certainly needs an equity raise (dilution), and 2029 production is far off — if lithium rolls over, the catalyst fizzles.

**2. HNRG (Hallador Energy) — a coal stock quietly becoming a data-center utility.** The market still prices HNRG like a dying coal miner, but it's converting its 1 GW Merom plant into behind-the-meter power for a data center — with the rare luxury of a **debt-free balance sheet** plus a forward-contracted revenue book (~$1.86B after a new 12-year capacity deal) worth roughly **2x its entire market cap**, so it can wait for the catalyst without dilution. Re-rating from a "coal" multiple (~9.5x) toward a "data-center power" multiple is the engine. **Biggest risk:** the data-center conversion never reaches a definitive PPA, leaving a single-plant Indiana coal IPP whose re-rate stalls near $25–27.

**3. EU (enCore Energy) — the uranium laggard that's actually profitable.** While the market bid UEC/UUUU/LEU into the stratosphere in 2025, enCore got left behind after a 2024 operational stumble — yet it's a *real ISR producer that returned to profit in 2026* with ~$96M liquidity, scaling a multi-satellite South-Texas hub into a uranium deficit. A $325M producer re-rating toward peers as it proves consistent output is a comfortable multi-bagger. **Biggest risk:** they've disappointed before — wellfield grades/recovery can underwhelm, plus a dilution history; if uranium fades from ~$85 the cheap producer stays cheap.

**4. AVIR (Atea) — a binary you're paid to hold.** ~$256M cash against a tiny enterprise value cushions the downside while you wait for a genuine binary: pivotal Phase 3 topline data for its HCV cure regimen, **due mid-2026** (imminent). Best-in-class data plausibly drives a strategic transaction at a multiple of today's price. **Biggest risk:** it's binary — an endpoint miss gaps it toward cash — and even on success, hepatitis C is a *cured, shrinking* market, which caps the prize (why I score upside 3–5x, not 10x).

**5. PACB (Pacific Biosciences) — a washed-out platform at the start of its consumables S-curve.** Left for dead after years of cash burn and flat revenue, PACB sits at ~$1.33 (barely off its low, −51% from its high) with **$276M cash** — while its new **Vega benchtop long-read sequencer** is driving instrument placements that pull through a high-margin consumables annuity (consumables already at records). If the razor-and-blades model inflects, a $400M company re-rates hard. **Biggest risk:** revenue still isn't growing — if Vega pull-through disappoints, the consumables thesis breaks and the market re-focuses on cash burn (and the convertible debt).

**6. SRTS (Sensus Healthcare) — a $73M company with a regulatory tailwind nobody's pricing.** CMS published the **first-ever dedicated CPT codes for SRT/IG-SRT skin-cancer treatment in 2026, with ~+300% on the per-fraction delivery code** — a direct boost to the economics of buying a Sensus system. The stock is near its 52-wk low after a brutal single-customer Q1, it's net-cash and FCF-positive, and modest order re-acceleration multiplies a tiny base. **Biggest risk:** customer concentration — Q1 showed one customer pulling back can halve revenue; capital-equipment sales are lumpy.
